# Ready-made characters for Remotion

Research snapshot, 9 November 2026. This is a review of source code and published capabilities. No candidate was installed or rendered in this pass.

## What looks wrong in the railway shot

In the supplied frame, Milo’s head is tipped upward, but the nose, the far eye, the glasses and the silhouette still describe one fixed three-quarter view. The contours close to the far eye are crowded, and the face reads as a drawing tilted toward the corner of the screen. The code confirms that the same face group rotates around a point on the lower head. Eyelids, pupils and the mouth animate inside that group, while the eye sockets, nose, glasses and jaw keep their fixed positions relative to each other. Changing the nose outline in the previous revision made more room around the eye. It did not supply a view of the face from a higher or lower angle. [Head implementation](../remotion/src/shots/prototype-railway-moth/MiloCel.tsx#L93) · [Shot timing](../remotion/src/shots/prototype-railway-moth/RailwayMoth.tsx#L88)

For a close-up, the choices are to draw and switch between complete head poses, or use a prepared rig that reshapes the face across authored view angles. Artwork can be raster or vector. A body rig can reuse a walk or jump; facial expression and head-turn controls still depend on the individual character. Live2D's authored X/Y keyforms and Rive's hand-drawn head-turn example demonstrate both approaches. [Live2D head-turn keyforms](https://docs.live2d.com/4.2/en/cubism-editor-manual/keyform-xydirection/) · [Rive character examples](https://rive.app/use-cases/character-animation)

## Fit for this repository

Use two separate checks. For the facial problem, use an authored Live2D sample or a suitably licensed rig and render a close-up of the eyes tracking an object, the head turning up and the mouth changing expression. For automated body motion, use an existing Rive rig to test walk, stop and look at a mark. A starter with only walk/run/idle can validate playback and seeking. It cannot validate a convincing head turn.

The proposed adapter reads one local character manifest with its available motions, expressions, supported facing angles and asset provenance. It maps shot cues to that character’s real controls and produces an alpha sequence or draws a reproducible frame inside the composition. Remotion keeps the camera edits, backgrounds, audio and MP4. For runtimes that advance through a state machine, test the same target frame in sequence and independently, since Remotion can render frames out of order.

After that works for one character, an MCP wrapper could expose `list_characters`, `describe_character`, `preview_pose` and `render_take`. It would wrap documented APIs and local assets. Adding a new character requires a compatible rig or its own motion mapping. A useful proof is the same character in a wide walking shot and a close-up, with an identical result when a selected frame is rendered twice.
