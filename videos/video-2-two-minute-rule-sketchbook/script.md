# The Two-Minute Rule — Sketchbook Edition

Format: 1920×1080 video · 60 seconds · Editorial sketchbook · No narration · No captions

This is a sketchbook reinterpretation of the existing motion-graphics video. It presents one
paraphrased idea from *Atomic Habits* by James Clear, is not a replacement for the book, and uses
no direct quotation.

## Chapter outline

1. **The problem (0:00–0:10)** — A hand-drawn mountain makes the starting threshold visible.
2. **The rule (0:10–0:20)** — An animated ink timer introduces the under-two-minute starter action.
3. **Make it tiny (0:20–0:30)** — Three ambitions are crossed out and translated into entrances.
4. **Lower the threshold (0:30–0:40)** — A drawn gate lifts so the character can begin moving.
5. **Momentum (0:40–0:50)** — The recurring idea-thread becomes an identity path.
6. **Try it now (0:50–1:00)** — Three checked sketch cards form the practical plan.

## On-screen copy

- Starting feels too big.
- We picture the entire mountain before taking the first step.
- Focus on the doorway. Make the first move obvious.
- The Two-Minute Rule
- Scale a new habit down to a starter version that takes under two minutes.
- Read every night → Read one page
- Work out → Put on your shoes
- Meditate daily → Take one slow breath
- Reduce the energy required to begin.
- Small starts create evidence.
- Each repetition is a vote for the person you want to become.
- Make starting the easiest part.
- Tiny enough to begin. Meaningful enough to repeat.
