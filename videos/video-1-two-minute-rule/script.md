# The Two-Minute Rule

Format: 1920×1080 video · 60 seconds · Motion graphics · No narration · No captions

This video presents one paraphrased idea from *Atomic Habits* by James Clear. It is not a replacement
for the book and does not use a direct quotation.

## Chapter outline

1. **The problem (0:00–0:10)** — Big habits create a high starting threshold.
2. **The rule (0:10–0:20)** — Scale a new habit down to a version that takes under two minutes.
3. **Make it tiny (0:20–0:30)** — Translate three familiar ambitions into tiny starter actions.
4. **Lower the threshold (0:30–0:40)** — Reducing friction makes the first movement easier.
5. **Momentum (0:40–0:50)** — Repetition creates evidence for a desired identity.
6. **Try it now (0:50–1:00)** — Choose, shrink, and show up.

## On-screen copy

- Starting feels too big.
- The goal is not to make the whole habit easy. It is to make the first move obvious.
- The Two-Minute Rule
- Scale a new habit down to a starter version that takes under two minutes.
- Read every night → Read one page
- Work out → Put on your shoes
- Meditate daily → Take one slow breath
- Reduce the energy required to begin.
- Small starts create evidence.
- Each repetition is a vote for the person you want to become.
- Make starting the easiest part.
- Tiny enough to begin. Meaningful enough to repeat.
